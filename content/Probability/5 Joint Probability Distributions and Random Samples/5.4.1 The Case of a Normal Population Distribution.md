The simulation experiment of Example 5.23 indicated that when the population distribution is normal, a histogram of $\bar{x}$ values for any sample size $n$ is well approximated by a normal curve.

> [!proposition] sum of sample from normal distribution
> Let ${X}_{1},{X}_{2},\ldots ,{X}_{n}$ be a random sample from a normal distribution with 
> - mean $\mu$
> - standard deviation $\sigma$. 
> 
> Then for any $n$, 
> - $\bar{X}$ is normally distributed with 
> 	- mean $\mu$
> 	- standard deviation $\sigma /\sqrt{n}$ 
> - as is ${T}_{o}$ with 
> 	- mean ${n\mu }$ 
> 	- standard deviation $\sqrt{n}\sigma$

We know everything there is to know about the $\bar{X}$ and ${T}_{o}$ distributions when the population distribution is normal. \
In particular, probabilities such as $P\left( {a \leq \bar{X} \leq b}\right)$ and $P\left( {c \leq {T}_{o} \leq d}\right)$ can be obtained simply by standardizing.
